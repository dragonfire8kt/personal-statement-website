Why make a Personal Statement website?

The Idea

One repeated concept in the advice I received about writing my Personal Statement was to stand out positively: there are many other applicants in the same position as me, so I should find a way be different. I would say that a Personal Statement website is different.

The Goal

I want to make it clear that my website is an experiment rather than a polished outcome. I did not create it to show that I am a perfect developer, because I am not; I built it to demonstrate that I am excited by Computer Science and am enthusiastic about exploring new topics and skills. I am proud of what I have achieved through this project, as the experience has taught me much that could not be learnt only in a classroom. I am prepared to apply what I have learnt in future endeavours, at university and in employment.

The Code

Please, look at the code! If you click on my name in the footer, you will be directed to the GitHub page where I have shared my code online. I understand that GitHub is used by developers to collaborate on projects, manage code repositories, control versions, and share successes and problems, etc: by storing my code there, I hope to gain useful experience working with a tool that I may encounter in future.

Key Development Points

There is too much to say about the development of my website to possibly mention it all here, but here are some interesting/useful points:

  • I chose to use HTML, CSS and JavaScript to program this website because exploring these languages beyond the A-level curriculum (in which I am taught the basics of each) will benefit my school work and improve my general programming ability through developing transferable skills.
  • I am hosting the website on my home Apache server.
  • I implemented a CI/CD pipeline.
  • I began by creating something rough with minimal functionality: I started with the basic layout of pages, then added increasingly complex features.
  • I chose to develop in Visual Studio Code - this is the IDE I used at my work experience placement in Digital Services at AUB, so I wanted to experiment with it further. I also know that VS Code is easily integrated with GitHub, providing greater suitability.
  • I used AI (a combination of Microsoft and GitHub Copilots) to assist in solving problems and help write some sections of the code. There is more information on my use of AI later.
  • I ensured, where I could, that the code is as maintainable as possible and open to scalability.

My Use of AI

A lot of my supercurricular activity has mentioned AI: my use of AI, especially in this project, has been guided by the advice of the software development team during my Digital Services work experience, multiple online lectures and videos, and the book "AI Demystified" by Antonio Weiss, among other sources. With it being such a talked-about developing technology, I wanted to make sure I considered how I could use AI in my website's development.

In the end, I used it to assist in programming some of the more-complicated features which would otherwise have taken me significantly longer to understand and implement. I also used it to aid the completion of repetitive tasks, saving time.

My use of AI in this project has been notably similar to some stages (particularly stage 2) of the AI-Assisted Development Lifecycle explained by THIS website.

Overall, I aimed to use AI responsibly, without letting it do all of the work. I always had control of how AI interacted with my code and I ensured that I understood every line it gave me. Using AI to assist in some areas of the program improved the efficiency of development and taught me more valuable content than only research could have.

In Conclusion

This website has helped me explore more deeply concepts covered in school, during my work experience, and in supercurriculars such as lectures, videos and books. I have enjoyed the experience and hope that you have found this an interesting break from the generic personal statement.

Thank you,

Kaitlin Addis
